Has Noah's Ark been found?

No. In 2026 a team drilled the Durupınar formation, a boat-shaped mound near Mount Ararat, but lab results were still pending and claims that it is confirmed outrun the evidence. The episode looks at what Genesis actually describes.

From Episode 10: Where the Ark Rested. Host Daniel Voss with Dr. Marisol Vega, naval architect and maritime archaeologist.

What Do We Do With a Story Like This

Daniel: Okay. Let me try pulling it together, and you tell me where I'm overreaching.

Marisol: I'll be watching for it.

Daniel: One. Genesis describes a real ship. Proportions a naval lab tested and couldn't improve much. Two. The older flood stories exist — and their boats are a cube and a round basket. Genesis is the one that floats. Three. The Bible doesn't put the ark on a single famous peak. It says "the mountains of Ararat," a region, and it gives a detail — seventy-four days until the peaks showed — that actually rules out the most famous candidate. Four. This fall, people are drilling there, and the honest status is: lab results pending.

Marisol: And where you overreach —

Daniel: — here it comes.

Marisol: — is on the worldwide flood. That's where your reading and my field part ways, and I don't think a good ship design settles a geology question.

Daniel: That's fair. A good ship design doesn't settle it. But here's the part that matters most to me, and it's not the boat. It's the pitch. "Kaphar." The covering. There was one way to be saved from that water, and it was to be inside the ship, under the covering, through the one door God told Noah to put in its side. Jesus said the days before He returns will be like the days of Noah. People eating, drinking, getting married — and not knowing. The point of the ark was never the wood. It was the door.

Marisol: You know, if they do find wood in those samples, I'll be the first one on the plane.

Daniel: And if they don't?

Marisol: Then I'll still remember my professor. When you have no harbor, you build a box.

Daniel: And you get inside it.

Daniel: Somewhere in eastern Turkey, three hundred and sixty-six little tubes of mud and stone are waiting in a lab. Whatever they say, the door's still open, in the side of the ship, where it always was. We'll be back.
