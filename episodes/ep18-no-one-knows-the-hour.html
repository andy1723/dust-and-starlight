Is AI a sign of the end times?

What Do We Do with a Story Like This

Daniel: Let me try pulling it together. Tell me where I'm overreaching.

Adaeze: You know I will.

Daniel: Jesus told his followers not to be alarmed, not to be deceived, and that no one knows the day — not even him. Every generation since has had someone sure it was theirs: 1844, 1988, 2000, 2011. Now the newest prophets are scientists and engineers, with extinction odds on one side and a digital heaven on the other. Revelation's talking image is real Scripture, but its first readers would have thought of emperor statues and temple tricks, and its point is worship — who you bow to — not which gadget. Some AI risks are real and worth working on. And the Bible's answer to "when" is the same every time: be found faithful. Feed the cat. Plant yams. Save for college.

Adaeze: My refinements. One — Matthew twenty-four thirty-four is still hard. Your reading is serious, but it's not the only one, and skeptics aren't crazy to raise it. Two — the AI risk isn't a prophecy, it's a forecast, and forecasts can be worked on. That's different from a date-setter. Some of these people are trying to prevent the thing they fear. I respect that. And three...

Daniel: Three?

Adaeze: Three is that I've spent twenty years studying people waiting for the end. And the ones who came through it whole — the ones who didn't break — weren't the ones with the best timeline. They were the ones who kept loving the people in front of them. My grandmother. Your dad with the pancakes. I don't know what that proves. But I've noticed it.

Daniel: I think it proves he was right about the servant.

Adaeze: Thank you for having me, Daniel.

Daniel: Dr. Adaeze Nwosu, thank you. To everyone listening with one eye on the headlines: nobody knows the hour. Not the man on the radio, not the forecaster, not me. So take your sneakers off. Go plant something. And if the end comes, let it find you making pancakes for somebody you love. This has been Dust & Starlight.
