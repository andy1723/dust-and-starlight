When does human life begin?

Biologically, at fertilization: a new, living human organism with its own DNA begins to grow. The real debate is whether that life is a person, and the episode weighs what the Bible says, the 1.1 million U.S. abortions a year, the hard cases, and the forgiveness offered to everyone touched by abortion.

From Episode 20: Before I Formed You. Host Daniel Voss with Dr. Elena Marsh, obstetrician-gynecologist who has provided abortions for fifteen years.

What Do We Do with a Story Like This

Daniel: Let me try pulling it together, and you tell me where I'm wrong.

Elena: I'll try. I'm a little shaken, honestly.

Daniel: Biology says that at conception there's a living, human, developing organism. The real question is personhood. The commandment is against unjustly taking innocent human life — and the earliest Christians applied it to abortion and to abandoned babies, two thousand years ago. The numbers are enormous: over a million a year, over sixty million since nineteen seventy-three. Most women aren't choosing it out of cruelty, but out of fear and poverty and abandonment — and that's a call to the church, not just to the women. The hard cases are real and should be handled with care, not slogans. And for anyone who has been part of an abortion — the woman, the father, the doctor — there is no condemnation in Christ. None.
